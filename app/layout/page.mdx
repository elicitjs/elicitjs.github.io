# Layout

<Lead>A **layout** settles marks in pixel space after an edit commits, then writes the settled positions and sizes back into the dataset. Declare layouts in `layout: [...]` on the `Elicit` spec. The root layouts work on any table: `layout.dodge` keeps marks from overlapping, `layout.row`, `layout.column`, `layout.grid` and `layout.pack` arrange them into slots or clusters, `layout.tidy` lines a hand layout up, and `layout.custom` runs your own pass. A layout that needs a structure is scoped to it: `layout.network.layered`, `tree` and `force` place a [network](/marks/network)'s nodes by its links, and `layout.hierarchy.contain` sizes containers in a [hierarchy](/marks/hierarchy).</Lead>

<ApiReference entries={api} />

<Section id="model" title="Layout vs constraints">

[Constraints](/constraints) and layouts enforce two different kinds of rule:

- A **constraint** is a pure-data invariant (`maintainSum`, `clamp`, `ordering`, `constraints.contain`). It runs on committed data rows and applies across every chart sharing a dataset.
- A **layout** works in **pixel space**. It measures each mark's drawn box (its width, height, radius or label extent), resolves overlaps, slots or container fits, and inverts the settled pixels back into the data columns within the same undo step.

While you drag, overlapping or slotted neighbours preview live and ease with `motion`; the settled positions commit once on release.

</Section>

<Section id="dodge" title="dodge — keep marks apart">

`layout.dodge({ gap, where, in })` pushes overlapping marks apart by `gap` pixels (default `8`). It works on any table of positioned marks — cards, scatter points, or network nodes — with no hierarchy required.

```js
layout: [
  layout.dodge({ gap: 10 }),
]
```

When you drop a mark on top of another, the dropped mark holds its place and the others move the shortest distance that clears it. Locked rows stay put.

/>

</Section>

<Section id="arrange" title="row, column, grid and pack">

Arrangement rules place marks into slots or tight clusters:

- `layout.row({ gap, in, where })` arranges left to right, wrapping at the available width.
- `layout.column({ gap, in, where })` arranges top to bottom, wrapping at the available height.
- `layout.grid({ columns, gap, in, where })` places equal cells in reading order.
- `layout.pack({ gap, keep, in, where })` draws marks together until they touch, edge to edge for boxes and circumference to circumference for circles.

On a flat table or a network, every row in the table takes part.

<Example
  code={flatPack}
  title="Packed circles on a flat table"
  blurb="layout.pack({ gap: 4 }) gathers dragged circles into a tight cluster."
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> any circle to the edge of the cluster and let go: it slides in to touch.
    </>
  )}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="composing" title="Composing levels in a hierarchy">

In a [hierarchy](/marks/hierarchy), `in` scopes each rule to a level of the tree:

- `"containers"` — inside every container (the default for `row`, `column`, `grid` and `pack`).
- `"roots"` — top-level nodes only.
- `"all"` — both roots and containers (the default for `dodge`).
- `(parent, i, rows) => boolean` — only inside containers matching the predicate.

Combine rules in `layout: [...]` to arrange roots one way and container contents another. `layout.hierarchy.contain({ padding, fit })` accepts `fit: true`, `"x"`, `"y"`, or `false` so containers can grow on a single axis while holding a fixed width.

<Example
  code={boardTwoLevel}
  title="Lanes in a row, cards in a column"
  blurb='layout.row({ in: "roots" }) aligns lanes side by side; layout.column stacks cards and fit: "y" grows each lane vertically.'

  try={(

      <b>Drag</b> a lane left or right to reorder the columns. <b>Drag</b> a card within a lane
      or into another lane: the lane grows or shrinks vertically while keeping its width.
    </>
  )}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="network" title="Network layouts">

`layout.network.*` places nodes from their links. Each one works on the nodes table of a dataset with links: a network, or a [hierarchy](/marks/hierarchy) that declares a links table. On a dataset with no links it logs a warning and does nothing. `where` picks the nodes it places.

- `layout.network.layered({ direction, gap, rankGap, place, where })` draws ranks for flowcharts and causal maps. Every link runs one way (`direction`: `"down"`, `"right"`, `"up"` or `"left"`), and nodes in a rank are ordered to cut crossings. A cycle has to break somewhere, so one link in each loop points back.
- `layout.network.tree({ direction, gap, rankGap, place, where })` draws a tidy tree for trees and mind maps. Each parent sits centred on its children, and siblings keep the order you drew them in. `direction: "radial"` puts the root in the middle with a ring per depth. The root is a node no link points to. Links that make a cycle or give a node two parents are left out of the layout, with a warning.
- `layout.network.force({ length, gap, place, where })` spreads a concept map. Linked nodes sit about `length` px apart (default `100`), and other pairs sit farther apart the more links lie between them. The same rows always give the same drawing.

`gap` is the space in px between neighbouring boxes, and `rankGap` the space between ranks or depths.

<Section id="place" title="Placing new rows or every row">

`place` decides which rows a network layout moves.

- `place: "new"` (the default) positions only rows with no `x` or `y` yet: the seed, a `setData` call, or rows added from outside. Every row that has a position stays where it is, so a participant's arrangement is never redrawn.
- `place: "all"` redraws every row whenever the nodes or links change. It starts from the current drawing, so a dragged node changes its rank order, sibling order or starting point instead of springing back.

Locked rows stay put under both.

<Example
  code={networkForce}
  title="New concepts placed around the ones already drawn"
  blurb='layout.network.force({ length: 80 }) places the seven concepts with no position. The three placed ones stay where they are.'
  tall
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> any concept: nothing else moves.
    </>
  )}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="where" title="where — lay out some of the rows">

Every layout takes `where: (d, i, rows) => boolean`. It picks the rows the layout acts on, and the others stay where they are. On `layout.dodge` the picked rows dodge, on `row`, `column`, `grid` and `pack` they take the slots, on `tidy` they line up, and a network layout places them, leaving links to other nodes out of the layout. On `layout.hierarchy.contain` it picks the containers its `padding` and `fit` apply to, and `layout.custom` can change only the rows it picks. Give two layouts different `where` rules to lay out two parts of one diagram differently.

In a hierarchy, a container with `fit: true` grows round the nodes the layout places inside it. A node the layout moves carries its own contents.

<Example
  code={networkInHierarchy}
  title="Two groups, each with its own layout"
  blurb='layout.network.layered({ where: (d) => d.kind === "step" }) places the sign-up steps, and layout.network.tree({ where: (d) => d.kind === "topic" }) the help topics. The groups and the note are left where they are.'
  tall
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> a group: its nodes go with it. <b>Drag</b> a step away and let go: it goes back to its rank.
    </>
  )}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="explore" title="Layouts and link shapes together">

A layout places the nodes, and the link mark's `curve` draws the links between them. `sourceSide` and `targetSide` pin where a link leaves and arrives, so its ends follow the layout's direction. Links are drawn one at a time, so several can end at one point on a node, and long links in a layered drawing pass close to the nodes they skip.

<Section id="tidy" title="tidy — line a hand layout up">

`layout.tidy({ snap, gap, where, in })` keeps the arrangement you drew and straightens it. Centres within `snap` px of each other (default `16`) share a row or a column. Rows and columns that are nearly evenly spaced become exactly even. A mark that overlaps another and stands alone in its column moves to the nearest free cell in its row, keeping `gap` px clear (default `8`). It works on any table, and `in` scopes it in a hierarchy as it does `dodge`.

<Example
  code={tidyCards}
  title="A rough sketch lined up"
  blurb="layout.tidy({ snap: 20 }) snaps the cards into rows and columns and moves Docs off Ship."
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> a card near a row and let go: it lines up with it.
    </>
  )}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="custom" title="Custom pixel-space layouts">

`layout.custom(fn, { table? })` runs a pixel-space pass after the built-in rules. `fn` receives `{ boxes, rows, tree, width, height, scales, padding, locked, anchors, spacing, preview }` and mutates each box's `cx`, `cy`, `hw`, `hh` in place; the engine inverts the modified boxes back into data columns.

```js
layout: [
  layout.custom(({ boxes, width }) => {
    for (const box of boxes) {
      if (box) box.cx = Math.max(box.hw, Math.min(width - box.hw, box.cx));
    }
  }),
]
```

</Section>
