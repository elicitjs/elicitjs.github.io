# Track

<Lead>`track()` draws a value as a length along a route: a bar that wraps, coils or curls round a circle. The route is the `path` option, an SVG path. The `length` channel is how much of it is drawn, and `move()` on `length` drags the end of the bar along the route.</Lead>

<ApiReference entries={api} />

<Section id="route" title="A route for every row">

The schema's domain spans the whole route: the domain's maximum draws all of it, and half of it draws half. Rows read on one scale when every row's route is the same length.

`path` is in plot pixels. A function of the row, `(d, i, data) => string`, gives each row its own route. When `x`, `y` or `size` is bound, the path is in unit form and placed the way [`shape`](/marks/shape) places one: centred at `x`/`y`, fitted to a box of radius `size`, turned by `angle`. One path then draws a route for every row.

`move()` puts the end of the bar at the point of the route nearest the pointer. You can press anywhere on the route, even where no bar is drawn, so a bar at zero can still be grabbed. During a drag the end follows the route from where it was, so it doesn't jump to a part of the route that only passes close by. `slide()` on `length` also works: it adds to the value as you drag along one axis.

<Section id="lanes" title="Lanes and joins">

Each `M` in the path starts a **lane**. Only lanes hold value, so the gap from one lane to the next adds nothing to the length. `join` draws what connects them: `"round"` turns through a curve that leaves along one lane and arrives along the next, so two lanes running opposite ways get a half circle. `"straight"` draws a line across the gap. `"none"` (the default) leaves it empty. A join is drawn once the bar reaches the next lane.

A route with one lane, such as a coil with square corners, needs no `join`: every pixel of it holds value.
