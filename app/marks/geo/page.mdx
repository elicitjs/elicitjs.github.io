# Geo

<Lead>Geo marks place rows by longitude and latitude through a chart-level `projection`, instead of x/y scales. Frame any place by fitting GeoJSON or a lon/lat bounding box in `projection.domain`, or by passing a live `d3.geo*` instance with your own `center` and `scale`. `geoBasemap({ geojson })` draws vector maps and `geoTile()` lays Web Mercator raster tiles behind your marks.</Lead>

<ApiReference entries={api} />

<Section id="locations" title="Framing a location">

A bare projection name such as `projection: "mercator"` fits the whole world. Point the map at a specific place in one of three ways:

- **Fit a GeoJSON object:** `projection: { type: "mercator", domain: geojson, inset: 6 }`. Any FeatureCollection, Feature, or Geometry fills the plot frame. Note that `d3-geo` treats clockwise exterior rings as the interior of a polygon (opposite to RFC 7946), so a reversed polygon zooms out to the globe and logs a warning.
- **Fit a lon/lat bounding box (no GeoJSON needed):** pass a `MultiPoint` of the southwest and northeast corners as `domain`: `domain: { type: "MultiPoint", coordinates: [[west, south], [east, north]] }`. A `MultiPoint` has no ring winding, so it fits any rectangle reliably.
- **Centre on `[lon, lat]` and zoom level:** pass a live `d3.geoMercator()` instance directly as `projection`. Options such as `center` and `scale` belong on a `d3.geo*` instance rather than the plain `projection` options object. For Web Mercator tiles, slippy-map zoom level `z` converts directly to scale with `(256 * 2 ** z) / (2 * Math.PI)`, and `translate([innerWidth / 2, innerHeight / 2])` puts `center` in the middle of the plot.

For globe or regional projections such as `"orthographic"` or `"conic-conformal"`, pass `rotate: [-lon, -lat]` in the `projection` options object to centre on `[lon, lat]`.

`lon` and `lat` are projected raw rather than through x/y scales, so their `schema` entries only need `{ type: "quantitative" }`. Add a `clamp` constraint only when you want to bound where pins may be dragged.

<Section id="zoom-pan" title="Zooming and panning">

To zoom and pan a map, pass a live `d3.geoMercator()` instance as `projection` and mutate its `scale` and `translate` from two `custom` edits:

- **Wheel zoom** (`gesture: "wheel", pick: "plane", viewport: true`): scale `proj` about `ctx.pointer` so the geographic point under the cursor stays fixed.
- **Drag pan** (`gesture: "drag", pick: "pan", viewport: true`): shift `proj.translate()` by the pointer travel from `ctx.session.panFrom`. Dragging open map pans the view; dragging a pin, vertex, or study box still edits that mark.

Attach both edits to the same mark that carries any other plane edit (such as `edit.geo.create()` on `geoPoint`), or to `geoTile()` / `geoBasemap()` when no other mark takes plane gestures. Set `margins: { top: 0, right: 0, bottom: 0, left: 0 }` so tiles, vector outlines, and marks clip cleanly to the frame. Every mark and `edit.geo.*` gesture reads the live projection on each render, so raster tiles load at the new zoom level and edits stay aligned.

<Section id="tiles" title="Real basemaps (OSM raster tiles)">

`geoTile()` lays a raster basemap — a pyramid of 256px tiles from a tile server — behind your marks. Tiles are pre-rendered in Web Mercator, so it requires `projection: "mercator"` or a `d3.geoMercator()` instance; other projections draw nothing and log a warning instead of a misaligned map. Lon/lat marks and `edit.geo.*` gestures work the same on top, because they use the same projection. Every tile service requires attribution as a licence condition, and `geoTile()` draws it by default. Public OSM tiles are rate-limited, so in production point `url` at your own server or a paid provider.

<Section id="labels" title="Label and rename places">

`geoText` is a text mark positioned by the projection instead of x/y scales. Every other text behaviour carries over: `editText()` to retype the content, `cycle()` to advance a categorical label, `rotate()` on `angle`. Pair it with a `geoPoint` that reads the same rows, and the label follows when you drag the dot. `dx`/`dy` offset the text from its anchor and leave the lon/lat unchanged.

<Section id="connected" title="Connected scatter over the map">

Give `geoLine` `lon`/`lat` channels instead of `coordinates` and it connects the dataset's rows into one path, the geographic counterpart of `path`. Draw the points with a separate `geoPoint` mark over the same rows; dragging a stop re-derives the trail. Rows connect in array order (`connect: "sequence"`, the default). An `order` channel sorts them by a column, and a `series` channel draws one trail per group.

<Section id="rects" title="Geographic rectangles">

`geoRect` boxes are aligned to lon/lat, so they can look slightly curved under Mercator. Drag an edge or corner to reshape a box, drag its body to move it, or click empty space to add another. The zone you press in holds for the whole drag. The cursor shows the zone before you press.
